CaseReady 1.0.0 is the first public release of the guided support-intake application.

## Included

- A five-stage conversation that begins with the customer’s objective and records impact, observed behaviour, environment, evidence, prior checks, ownership and the exact request for the next team.
- Four deterministic views from one completed intake: the recorded facts, a customer confirmation, an internal support brief and an engineering escalation.
- An impact-and-urgency severity suggestion that remains editable.
- Explicit local case-file saving and opening, plus Markdown copy and download actions for each output.
- A focused warning for credential-like text, private keys and full payment-card numbers.
- Three editable fictional scenarios and five committed example exports generated by the application logic.
- Responsive desktop and mobile layouts with keyboard-visible controls, validation messages and distinct customer and internal output treatments.
- Automated checks for the output rules, example reproducibility, unfinished copy, rendered metadata and the production build.

## Privacy boundary

CaseReady has no backend, account system, analytics integration or automatic draft persistence. Answers remain in the active browser tab unless the operator explicitly downloads a local case file.

## Verification

The public deployment was exercised through the full fictional payment-and-order scenario at desktop and mobile sizes. The final capture covered the home page, blank validation, the sensitive-data warning, completed intake, customer confirmation, support brief and engineering escalation. It reported no browser errors and no mobile horizontal overflow.

## Known limitations

- There is no shared queue, cross-device synchronisation or collaborative editing.
- The application does not integrate with ticketing, monitoring or customer-data systems.
- Severity rules do not replace an organisation’s incident policy.
- Sensitive-data detection is intentionally narrow.
- Outputs still require operator review and cannot diagnose the underlying fault.
- Downloaded files are unencrypted and remain under the operator’s control.
